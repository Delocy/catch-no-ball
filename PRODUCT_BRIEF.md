# Catch No Ball: product brief
**In one line:** A newcomer in their first months at a Singapore office catches what colleagues mean, tone included, as it is said.

## Problem
Foreign professionals in their first three months at a Singapore office lose what colleagues mean when talk relaxes into Singlish at meetings and lunch. Particles and slang carry tone standard English cannot express, so a reluctant "can lor" sounds like a firm yes. Rather than keep asking, they nod along and misread what was meant, which brings wrong expectations, rework and a slower fit with the team.

## Evidence
- You saw a colleague ask what "lor" meant after a meeting, having read a reluctant yes as a firm yes.
- Not checked yet: the 4 to 5 out of 10 baseline is your guess, not a measurement.
- Not checked yet: whether they would use a phone in a meeting.

## Success
- **Metric:** Out of 10 Singlish sentences in a meeting, how many the newcomer understands correctly, tone included.
- **Today:** 4 to 5, your guess, not measured.
- **Target:** not set
- **Must not get worse:** not set

## Riskiest bet
Newcomers will be comfortable looking at their phone for subtitles mid-meeting, and won't find it rude.
- **Test:** Two Singaporean teammates hold a 3-minute Singlish mock meeting; a third types plain-English translations into a shared doc; 3 to 5 non-Singaporean classmates follow on their phone. Ask if they would use it in a real meeting, during or right after.
- **Pass mark:** 3 of 5 would use it, during or right after, and at most 1 finds it too awkward. If most say only after, build the recap, not live subtitles.
- **Result:** not run yet

## First version
1. As a newcomer, I start listening and tell the table I'm using a translator. Done when a "Translating" label shows.
2. As a newcomer, I glance once and keep following. Done when a card shows what was said, what it meant and the tone.
3. As a newcomer, I look only when there is something to read. Done when plain-English sentences show faded.

## Walkthrough
1. They open Catch No Ball from the shortcut, pick "Office meeting", tap Listen and tell the table they're using a translator.
2. A colleague says "Eh the deadline confirm cannot shift one lah". A card shows the words, the meaning and a "firm" tone label.
3. They tap "siam" for "skip / avoid", star it, and at Stop see "You caught 8 Singlish terms".

If it goes wrong: "Couldn't catch that" with a box to type what they heard, both readings when the meaning is unclear, and "Subtitles paused, reconnecting" when the connection drops.

## Not building
- Anything outside Singlish in Singapore offices.
- Translating sentences already in plain English.
- Lessons, quizzes or transcripts.

## Open questions
- What target counts as working, and what must not get worse?
- Is 4 to 5 out of 10 close to real?
- Will Singlish recognition hold up at a noisy lunch?
